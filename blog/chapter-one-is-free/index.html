What's actually in chapter one

Why AI hands you mush, and the three things you change to stop it. Not seven tips. Three levers, and they are the same three every single time: tell it which seat it's sitting in, tell it plainly what answers you refuse to accept, and tell it the exact shape you want the answer in.

Then one closing line that I put on every prompt that matters: name what would have to be true for you to be wrong. Confidence is a tone these things perform on demand. A falsifier is something you can go check.

You can use all of that today, in whatever chat window you already have open, without buying anything from me. That is deliberate. If chapter one doesn't change your Tuesday, the other fourteen aren't going to either, and I'd rather you find that out for free.

Why I'm giving away the chapter with the audio in it

Most people selling a course give you a sales page and ask you to trust the sales page. I'd rather you read the actual writing and hear the actual voice before any money is discussed. If somebody has read a chapter and listened to it and then decides to buy, nobody is surprised later by what they got.

That's the whole reason the free chapter exists. It isn't generosity. It's the cheapest honest way to sell something.

Who's writing this

I'm one human. My company has 27 AI employees with real job descriptions — each one owns a single thing, has a written list of what it may never touch, and hands a decision up to me instead of guessing. AI produces about 95% of our work product, carries about 90% of the labor, and holds 0% of the authority. Those numbers came out of our own ledgers, not out of a feeling.
