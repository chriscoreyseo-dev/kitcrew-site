## What they sell

An **Influencer Agent** that runs a campaign end to end from one conversation:
matches creators from its own network, runs outreach, negotiates and locks
rates, tracks deliverables, routes content approval, reports performance, and
pays the creator automatically once work is confirmed.

It sits inside a broader "AI CMO" product that also covers SEO, AI-search
visibility scoring, and social engagement on X, LinkedIn and Reddit. For our
purposes only the Influencer Agent matters.

## The part that actually matters — the claims rider

Paid creators promoting KitCrew to a network-marketing-adjacent audience create
exposure we do not have when our own agents produce the content. We control our
agents by construction. A third-party creator we control **only by contract.**

Any engagement must carry, in writing:

1. **No income or earnings representations of any kind**, in caption, voiceover,
   on-screen text, thumbnail, or comment replies. This binds the creator, not
   just the brief.
2. **Approval before publish, on every asset**, including edits and reposts.
   Never a blanket pre-approval.
3. **Material-connection disclosure** on every piece — the relationship is paid,
   and it has to be visible, not buried.
4. **No implied-outcome imagery** — dashboards, ledgers, lifestyle shots
   standing in for earnings, before/after framing.
5. **Comment-reply scope.** A creator answering *"how much can I make?"* under
   their own sponsored post is an income claim attributed to us. Either they
   route it to us or they don't reply at all.
6. **Takedown on demand**, at our sole discretion, with the fee still owed.
7. **We hold the compliance decision**, not the vendor and not the creator.

Point 5 is the one most likely to be missed in a standard influencer agreement
and the most likely to actually happen.

## Other diligence before engaging

- **Audience fit.** Their network was built for startup/SaaS brands. Whether it
  reaches network marketers and affiliates at all is unestablished.
- **Channel gap.** Live on X. Our audience is not primarily on X.
- **Payment rails.** They pay creators; confirm who is the merchant of record
  and where liability sits if a creator is paid for non-compliant content.
- **Member-side conflict.** If any creator is also a distributor, their own
  company's social policy may bar the promotion. Their problem contractually,
  our problem reputationally.
